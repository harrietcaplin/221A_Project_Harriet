Hypothesis 1

Will political alignment will correlate to gender equality attitudes?

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Political Alignment ('cot_politics'): A categorical variable with 5 alignment categories: Far Left, Center Left, Center, Center Left, Far Left
  • Gender Equality Attitudes ('viewtotal'): Composite score of 3 questions reflecting gender equality attitudes on a scale from 1-7 for each question, 3-21 for the total attitude score. This item has been reverse coded so that a lower score reflects more traditional gender views and a higher score reflects more progressive ones.

Graph

Figure 1 shows the distribution of points by political alignment. The half-violin shows the shape of the distribution, the box summarizes the median and quartiles, and the dots represent individual responses.

Observed trend

Code 
h1Data$cot_politics <- as.numeric(factor(h1Data$cot_politics, levels=c("Far Left",
                                                                       "Center Left",
                                                            "Center", "Center Right",
                                                            "Far Right"), labels=c(1:5)))
#| label: correlation
r <- cor(h1Data$cot_politics, h1Data$viewtotal, use = "complete.obs")
round(r, 2)
[1] -0.26

The correlation was {-0.26} round(r, 2), indicating a weak negative association. Respondents who identified with more right-wing politics hold more traditional gender beliefs.

H2. Social Media Use and Belief in the Importance of Attractiveness

Does social media use correlate with how people rate the importance of attractiveness?

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Social Media Usage (‘Time_social_media_1’): How much time participants report spending on social media per day (minutes).

  • Importance of Attractiveness (Attract_importance_1): The importance a respondent places on their own physical appearance rated on a scale from 1-9 (1 being Not At All Important and 9 being Extremely Important)

Graph

Figure 2 shows the relationship between time spent on social media and belief in the importance of one’s own attractiveness. Points are jittered to make overlapping responses easier to see; the recorded scores are unchanged. The line summarizes the linear trend.

Code 
ggplot(h2Data, aes(x = Time_social_media_1, y = Attract_importance_1)) +
  geom_jitter(width = .12, height = .04, alpha = .04, size = .6) +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm") +
  labs(x = "Time Spent on Social Media Per Day (minutes)",
       y = "Importance of Attractiveness (1–9)") +
  theme_minimal()
`geom_smooth()` using formula = 'y ~ x'

The correlation was {0.13} round(r, 2), indicating a weak positive association. Respondents who reported spending more time on social media placed more importance on their attractiveness.
